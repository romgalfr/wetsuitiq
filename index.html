Accuracy

What each brand's chart really measures

A fit tool is only as precise as the chart behind it. This table shows which measurements each brand's chart uses. When a brand doesn't publish a measurement, this page ignores it instead of guessing.

How to measure

Height

Barefoot, back to a wall, heels together, looking straight ahead.

Weight

Use your current weight, ideally first thing in the morning without shoes.

Chest

Tape level under the armpits across the fullest part of the chest, arms relaxed. Women: bust at the fullest point.

Waist

At the narrowest part of your torso, around navel height, tape snug but not squeezing.

Hip

Around the fullest part of the hips and seat, feet together. Used by 2XU women's and TYR.

Torso

Do suits usually feel short or long on you for your height? Only shapes the advice. No chart uses it.

Trying it on

Three fit checks when it arrives

Firm on land

A triathlon wetsuit feels tighter than anything else you've worn in the water. You should still take a full breath. If you can't, size up.

No loose neoprene

Pinch the torso and thighs. Loose material or air pockets at the lower back mean the suit is too big and will pump water.

Check the shoulders

Raise your arms in a freestyle motion. Dragging at the shoulders or a choking collar points to another size or cut.

Questions

Common questions

How does the fit score work?

Each of your measurements is compared with each size's range on the brand's chart. A measurement inside the range costs nothing. One outside it costs more the further out it is. Chest counts most, then weight, then height and waist, then hip. A measurement a chart doesn't publish is skipped, and each brand shows a confidence level based on how many of your measurements its chart can use and how reliable its chart source is. The lowest total wins.

What if I fall between two sizes?

Read the ranked sizes and the brand's own advice. 2XU, ROKA and Blueseventy suggest the smaller size when you fit two. HUUB suggests going up and says weight matters more than height. Zone3's "Tall" and "Medium Large" cuts are there for builds that don't match the standard sizes.

Why do some brands show a warning?

Sailfish and HUUB publish their official charts only as images that I could not read, so those two use retailer charts. ROKA publishes a height-by-weight grid, so its ranges here are approximate. Always confirm on the brand's own chart before ordering.

Can I return a suit that doesn't fit?

Returns are set by each brand or seller. Zone3 states an exchange policy on its sizing page. Read the current policy before you order.
